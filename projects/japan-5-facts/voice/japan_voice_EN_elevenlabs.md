```
A cat stationmaster. A volcano inside a volcano. Welcome to Japan. <break time="1.2s" />
Japan's official island count, published in twenty twenty-three, was fourteen thousand, one hundred and twenty-five. Better mapping changed the count. The country didn't suddenly grow. <break time="1.2s" />
Aogashima looks like a tiny green island. Look closer: a smaller volcanic cone sits inside a much larger caldera. It's a volcano inside a volcano — and people live on the island. <break time="1.2s" />
In two thousand seven, Kishi Station gave a cat named Tama the title of stationmaster. She greeted passengers and became the railway's famous mascot. Visitors came just to meet her. <break time="1.2s" />
These aren't creatures or sculptures. They're trees. In the Zao mountains, freezing droplets coat the branches with ice. Snow builds up on top, creating the shapes people call snow monsters. <break time="1.2s" />
And in Susami, you can post a postcard underwater. About ten metres down, a red mailbox stands on the seabed. Special postcards go in, and are collected for delivery. <break time="1.2s" />
Which country should we explore next? Tell us in the comments!
```
Проверь произношение: Aogashima (а-о-га-си-ма), Kishi, Tama, Zao (дза-о), Susami (су-са-ми).
