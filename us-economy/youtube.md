## Description
Can 50 countries together match the United States economy?

Fifty flags. One growing GDP counter. And an American benchmark of roughly $30.8 trillion.

In this 8-minute 21-second animated world map comparison, fifty selected countries join the team one at a time. We begin with smaller economies, then move through larger contributions from countries including the Netherlands, Türkiye, Australia, South Korea, Spain, Brazil, Canada, France, and the United Kingdom.

Watch the map fill up as the combined total moves through its biggest checkpoints. Forty countries reach about a quarter of the target. Later additions take the team past halfway—but will fifty countries be enough?

WHAT THIS VIDEO COMPARES
The United States’ nominal GDP against the combined GDP of a selected lineup, with country contributions, a running total, and a progress bar on a shared scale.

DATA AND METHODOLOGY
All figures use 2025 GDP in current US dollars from the World Bank’s World Development Indicators, indicator NY.GDP.MKTP.CD. GDP measures economic output over a year. This is a nominal-dollar comparison rather than a comparison of wealth, GDP per capita, purchasing power parity, or government spending.

The fifty countries were selected for this animated challenge. This is not the minimum number of countries required; a different lineup would produce a different answer. Adding existing national GDP figures is an arithmetic comparison, not a forecast of a real economic merger. Source values may be revised. Calculations use unrounded values; on-screen figures are rounded for readability.
